# Repair and Circular Economy
### Reviving Repair Culture through Community Mapping

**Sustainability Action Project | Azim Premji University | 2026**

## Project Overview

With the rise of fast fashion, mass production and convenience-driven consumption, replacing damaged goods has increasingly become an alternative to repairing them. This contributes to growing waste generation, shorter product lifespans and challenges to the livelihoods of traditional repair workers.

This project aims to promote circularity by encouraging individuals to repair and reuse everyday goods rather than discard them. It seeks to make repair a convenient, accessible and habitual practice while supporting the livelihoods of local repair service providers, such as cobblers, tailors and bicycle repairers.

The student community at Azim Premji University served as a pilot for this initiative, with a broader vision of developing a city-wide repair network across Bengaluru.

## Project Objectives

- Promote circular consumption practices among college students.
- Extend the lifespan of everyday goods through repair and reuse.
- Encourage a behavioural shift from replacing damaged products to repairing them.
- Reduce unnecessary consumption and waste generation.
- Develop an accessible, map-based directory of local repair service providers.
- Bridge the information gap between consumers and repair workers.
- Improve the visibility and livelihood opportunities of informal repair workers whose occupations face increasing pressure from fast fashion and disposable consumption.
- Explore the scalability of a community-based repair network across Bengaluru.

### My Individual Literature Review: Socio-economic Conditions of Cobblers

My research focused on understanding the social and economic realities of cobblers, particularly those operating within India's informal economy. Through a review of academic papers, field-based studies and media reports, I examined the challenges faced by these workers and how changing consumption patterns threaten their livelihoods.

#### Historical and Social Background

- Historical association of leatherwork and cobbling with communities such as the Mochi, Muchi and Madiga.
- Caste-based discrimination and the social marginalisation of traditional repair workers.
- Rural-to-urban migration and dependence on informal employment.

#### Economic Conditions and Changing Consumption Patterns

- **Pune, Maharashtra:** A study by Vishal More (2020) examined how age, education and work experience influence cobblers' earnings. The study highlighted irregular incomes, differences in earnings across age groups and migration from drought-prone rural regions.
- **Karnataka:** A Deccan Herald report (2018) documented how cobblers diversified their livelihoods by selling belts, bags and umbrellas alongside providing footwear repair services.
- **Bengaluru:** Visual documentation of roadside cobblers highlighted their craftsmanship and continued reliance on traditional repair techniques.

These studies were examined in the context of mass production, fast fashion, inexpensive footwear, declining repair culture and limited access to formal credit.

#### Gender Inequalities

- **Jaipur, Rajasthan:** A study by Jeevika examined female cobblers who had migrated from rural Rajasthan. It highlighted gender-based income disparities, limited access to formal banking, financial insecurity and harassment experienced by women working as street vendors.

#### Occupational Health Hazards

- **Bangladesh:** A study examined musculoskeletal disorders among cobblers, particularly lower back pain and neck strain associated with prolonged sitting and poor working conditions.
- **Kolkata, West Bengal:** A similar study reported musculoskeletal ailments among 45% of the cobblers studied.

### Research Gaps and Relevance to the Project

The combined literature review identified several gaps:

- Limited documentation of the lived experiences of informal repair workers.
- Insufficient quantification of the environmental benefits of everyday repair.
- Limited understanding of repair behaviour among younger consumers.
- Inadequate recognition and documentation of traditional repair knowledge.
- Limited exploration of inclusive digital platforms connecting consumers with informal repair workers.

These findings informed the design of our campus-level sustainability intervention. By improving the accessibility and visibility of local repair services, the project aims to simultaneously promote responsible consumption and support the livelihoods of repair workers.


## Part 2: Primary Data Collection and Community Mapping

### Campus as a Pilot Study

Azim Premji University was selected as the initial testing ground for the initiative. The campus provided an opportunity to understand consumption behaviour, identify barriers to repair and explore how improved access to local repair services could encourage more sustainable practices.

### Student Survey

A questionnaire was developed to understand student consumption patterns, attitudes towards repair and barriers to accessing repair services.

Over 100 responses were collected.

Key barriers identified included:

- Distance and limited accessibility to repair shops.
- Lack of awareness of nearby repair service providers.
- Limited tools and resources for self-repair.
- Time constraints and inconvenient shop timings.
- Concerns regarding repair costs and quality.

### Fieldwork and Repair Shop Mapping

Field visits were conducted in Sarjapur to identify and document local repair service providers.

The initial mapping exercise documented 13 repair providers:

| Repair category | Number documented |
|---|---:|
| Tailors | 7 |
| Bicycle repair shops | 3 |
| Watch repair shops | 2 |
| Cobblers | 1 |
| Total | 13 |

The fieldwork formed the foundation for a proposed digital directory that would allow students to easily locate nearby repair services whenever an item needed fixing.

The group also engaged with the university's Sustainability Committee to understand existing repair initiatives, including RRR Maadi.

## Proposed Intervention: A Digital Repair Directory

The project proposes an interactive, map-based platform connecting consumers with local repair service providers.

The platform would enable users to:

- Discover repair shops near their location.
- Search for vendors based on the type of repair required.
- Access information about available services, shop timings and locations.
- Contribute information about previously undocumented repair shops.
- Make more informed decisions about repairing rather than replacing products.

Repair workers would also be able to register their businesses, list their services and update their information, improving their digital visibility and access to potential customers.

By reducing the effort required to find repair services, the platform aims to make repair a more convenient and habitual choice.

## Future Scope: Scaling Across Bengaluru

While the initial project focused on the university community, its long-term vision extends beyond the campus.

The proposed repair directory could be developed into a city-wide platform connecting residents with repair service providers across Bengaluru.

Future developments could include:

- Expanding field-based mapping across different neighbourhoods.
- Developing a mobile-friendly interactive repair map.
- Enabling repair workers to independently register and manage their listings.
- Providing assisted registration for vendors with limited digital access.
- Enabling community-led data collection and verification.
- Partnering with universities, residential communities and local organisations.
- Establishing recurring repair cafés and community repair events.
- Evaluating changes in consumer repair behaviour following the intervention.
- Assessing the impact on repair workers' customer base and income.
- Quantifying potential waste reduction, cost savings and environmental benefits.

The long-term goal is to create an accessible urban repair ecosystem that makes repairing everyday goods a practical alternative to replacing them.

## Equity and Environmental Justice

The project recognises that circular economies depend significantly on the labour of informal workers, many of whom experience economic insecurity, social marginalisation and limited institutional support.

The proposed platform would therefore prioritise informed consent, inclusive participation, accessibility and repair workers' control over their publicly displayed information.
